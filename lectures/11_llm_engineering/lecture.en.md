## Why this matters

Decoding strategy, prompt format, memory, and time budget all affect the resulting system. These engineering decisions are separate from architecture choice.

## Intuition

A language model calculates one score for every vocabulary token. The scores describe a possible next token rather than a complete answer.

Generation repeats the same cycle. A distribution is produced, a token is selected, the token enters the context, and the model performs another step.

❌ An autoregressive model generates the whole answer at once.

✅ Typical decoding creates one token at a time. Every step also depends on tokens previously generated by the model.

## Autoregressive factorization

For token identifiers $x_1,\ldots,x_T$ from a vocabulary of size $V$, the chain rule gives

$$P(x_1,\ldots,x_T)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}P(x_t\mid x_1,\ldots,x_{t-1}).$$

This is an identity for any joint distribution. The model assumption concerns whether a network with parameters $\theta$ can approximate every factor.

$$P_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t}),\qquad x_{<t}:=x_1,\ldots,x_{t-1}$$

Here, $x_t$ denotes an integer identifier rather than the vector from module 9. The pipeline contains $x_t$ as an identifier, $\mathbf e_t$ as an embedding, and $z_t$ as logits. The symbol $V$ still denotes vocabulary size.

## Logits and softmax

For context $x_{<t}$, the model produces one logit per token.

$$z_t=f_\theta(x_{<t})\in\mathbb R^V$$

Logits may be negative and do not sum to 1. Softmax converts them into a distribution.

$$\hat y_t=\operatorname{softmax}(z_t),\qquad \hat y_{t,i}=\frac{e^{z_{t,i}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{V}e^{z_{t,j}}}$$

Component $\hat y_{t,i}$ approximates $P_\theta(x_t=i\mid x_{<t})$.

❌ The most probable token at every step guarantees the best complete answer.

✅ The distribution concerns one step. Locally optimal choices need not form the globally most probable sequence.

## Temperature

Temperature scales logits before softmax.

$$\hat y_t^{(\tau)}=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{z_t}{\tau}\right),\qquad \tau>0$$

The symbol $\tau$ distinguishes temperature from sequence length $T$.

- For $\tau<1$, logit differences increase and the distribution becomes sharper.
- For $\tau>1$, the distribution becomes flatter.
- As $\tau\to0^+$, all mass concentrates on $\arg\max_i z_{t,i}$.

❌ Temperature increases the model's creativity.

✅ It is a mathematical transformation of the distribution, not a change in model knowledge. Temperature does not select a token. With `do_sample=False`, it does not affect the result because scaling does not change the argmax.

## Token selection

### Greedy decoding

Greedy decoding selects $x_t=\arg\max_i\hat y_{t,i}$ and is deterministic.

It does not guarantee the globally best sequence. In the notebook counterexample, $P(A)=0.5498$ and $P(B)=0.4502$, so the first step selects $A$. Next, $P(X\mid A)=P(Y\mid A)=0.5$, while $P(X\mid B)=0.99$.

Greedy decoding creates $(A,X)$ with probability $0.2749$. The sequence $(B,X)$ has a larger probability of $0.4457$, but the local decision discards it at the first step. The notebook confirms this by enumerating all four sequences.

### Sampling

Sampling draws $x_t\sim\hat y_t^{(\tau)}$. Without a fixed seed, the output may change. Setting the PyTorch seed provides reproducibility verified in the notebook.

### Top-k

Top-k retains the $k$ highest-scoring tokens, zeros the remaining probabilities, and renormalizes the distribution. The number of retained tokens is fixed.

### Top-p

Top-p sorts tokens in descending order and keeps the smallest leading set whose cumulative probability exceeds $p$. The number of tokens depends on distribution concentration.

For a concentrated distribution, top-p may retain one or two tokens. For a flat distribution, it retains more. Top-k does not adapt the set size in this way.

## Five-token example

For the vocabulary `cat, dog, house, sea, xyz123` and logits $z=[2.0,1.5,0.5,0.1,-1.0]$, softmax at $\tau=1$ gives

$$\hat y=[0.4928,0.2989,0.1100,0.0737,0.0245].$$

| $\tau$ | cat | dog | house | sea | xyz123 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $0.5$ | $0.6932$ | $0.2550$ | $0.0345$ | $0.0155$ | $0.0017$ |
| $1.0$ | $0.4928$ | $0.2989$ | $0.1100$ | $0.0737$ | $0.0245$ |
| $2.0$ | $0.3495$ | $0.2722$ | $0.1651$ | $0.1352$ | $0.0780$ |

Top-k with $k=3$ retains `cat`, `dog`, and `house`, producing $[0.5465,0.3315,0.1220,0,0]$ after renormalization.

The cumulative probabilities are $0.4928$, $0.7918$, $0.9017$, $0.9755$, and $1.0$. Top-p with $p=0.7$ retains `cat` and `dog`; with $p=0.9$, it also retains `house`.

## Subword tokenization

Three granularities have different costs.

- Characters need a small vocabulary but create very long sequences.
- Words shorten sequences but require an enormous, permanently incomplete vocabulary.
- Subwords encode common words as one token and split rare words into known pieces.

BPE starts from characters or bytes and repeatedly merges the most frequent adjacent pairs. This module uses a ready-made tokenizer trained for the selected model.

Every token has an identifier that selects an embedding row. Special tokens may include BOS, EOS, and PAD. `generate()` stops after producing EOS.

❌ BOS, EOS, and PAD are always three distinct tokens.

✅ In the selected model, `pad_token_id == eos_token_id`. Identifier meanings must be checked for the specific tokenizer.

❌ One token always corresponds to one word.

✅ An English sentence with 9 words has 10 tokens, while a Polish sentence with 7 words has 25 tokens. The same tokenizer produces about 3.6 tokens per Polish word because it was trained mainly on English text.

## Prefill and decode

Generation has two stages.

1. **Prefill** processes the complete prompt in one pass, parallel across positions. It produces next-token logits and, with `use_cache=True`, K and V values for the prompt.
2. **Decode** generates one token at a time. Every new token requires a separate pass and depends on the previous step.

Prefill is usually faster per token because it uses position parallelism. Decode remains sequential.

## KV cache

Without a cache, every step would recalculate K and V for all preceding positions. Under causal attention, these representations do not change and can be stored.

❌ The KV cache is persistent memory of model knowledge.

✅ The cache stores K and V only for the current inference run. Model knowledge resides in parameters $\theta$. The cache is a computational optimization and changes neither the mathematical output nor reasoning ability.

Its exact memory cost is

$$\text{bytes}=2\times N\times H_{kv}\times d_{\text{head}}\times T\times\text{bytes}_{\text{dtype}}.$$

$N$ is the number of blocks, $H_{kv}$ the number of K/V heads, $d_{\text{head}}$ the head dimension, and the factor 2 accounts for separate K and V tensors.

❌ The number of query heads always equals the number of K/V heads.

✅ The selected model has 9 Q heads and 3 K/V heads. In grouped-query attention, several query heads share one K/V head, reducing cache memory. The tensor has shape `(batch, 3, T, 64)`.

At $T=16$, the formula gives exactly 737,280 bytes, matching the actual size of the `past_key_values` tensors.

## Chat templates

An instruct model is trained on conversations in a specific format. The `system`, `user`, and `assistant` roles and delimiter tokens are stored in `tokenizer.chat_template`.

❌ A chat template is merely cosmetic formatting.

✅ The format is part of the model contract. A prompt outside the fine-tuning distribution can reduce quality substantially without raising an error.

An example in ChatML format is

```text
<|im_start|>system
You are a helpful AI assistant named SmolLM, trained by Hugging Face<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>user
What is the capital of France?<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>assistant
```

`add_generation_prompt=True` adds the start of an assistant message. `apply_chat_template()` inserts the required special tokens. Adding them manually a second time produces invalid formatting.

### Conversation history

The model is stateless between calls. Earlier messages must be included explicitly in the next input. The cost of every turn grows with the complete history up to the context limit.

## Context window

`max_position_embeddings` sets the maximum number of positions. The selected model has a limit of 8192.

❌ `max_length` and `max_new_tokens` mean the same thing.

✅ `max_length` limits the combined prompt and response. `max_new_tokens` limits only the new part. A long prompt reduces the space available for a response, and exceeding the window requires deliberate truncation.

## Base and instruct models

- A base model learns next-token prediction during pretraining.
- SFT further trains it on instruction-response pairs in a conversation format.
- Preference tuning, such as DPO, teaches it to prefer one response over another.

❌ An instruct model has a different architecture from its base model.

✅ The architecture remains the same. Additional training changes weights and behavior rather than layer count or attention mechanism.

## Hugging Face Transformers workflow

```python
from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM, AutoTokenizer

tokenizer = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(MODEL_ID, revision=REVISION)
model = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(MODEL_ID, revision=REVISION)

inputs = tokenizer.apply_chat_template(
    messages,
    tokenize=True,
    add_generation_prompt=True,
    return_tensors="pt",
    return_dict=True,
)
output = model.generate(**inputs, max_new_tokens=64, do_sample=False)
```

`AutoTokenizer` and `AutoModelForCausalLM` select an implementation from the model configuration. `revision` pins a specific weight commit. `pipeline("text-generation")` is convenient, but lower-level `generate()` exposes the inference stages more clearly.

## Latency, throughput, and cost

Latency is the wait for a result. Throughput measures tokens processed per second. High aggregate throughput does not guarantee low latency for one request.

❌ Shorter inference time means a better model.

✅ Runtime depends on model size, lengths, hardware, cache, and precision. Answer quality is a separate evaluation axis.

Without a cache, the cost of each new step grows with position. For a fixed model, this leads approximately to $O(T^2)$ over the complete decode. A cache avoids recomputation and leads to $O(T)$ while consuming additional memory.

## Quality evaluation and risks

Evaluation should separate instruction following, factual correctness, completeness, required format, and reproducibility. A subjective impression is insufficient.

The model in this module has 135 million parameters. It can produce fluent responses for simple tasks, but reliable multi-step reasoning and specialized knowledge should not be assumed.

Basic risks include:

- hallucinations, meaning plausible but false text,
- instability under sampling,
- sensitivity to prompt wording,
- prompt injection in untrusted data,
- disclosure of confidential content to an external service,
- restrictions from model and dataset licenses,
- treatment of generated content as a verified fact.

Successful code execution, grammatical text, and factual correctness are independent criteria. Each requires a separate check.

## Experiments and observations

Results come from the executed [`examples.en.ipynb`](examples.en.ipynb) on a CPU and do not form a universal benchmark.

### A. Tokenization

The English sentence reaches 0.90 words per token and the Polish sentence 0.28. The number `1234.56` splits into individual digits and a period. The `§` character produces the byte artifact `ĠÂ§`. Rare words split into subwords rather than `<UNK>`.

### B. Distribution and greedy generation

The prompt `The capital of France is` has 35 tokens after the chat template. Its top five next tokens are `The` at $0.3113$, `B` at $0.0674$, `"` at $0.0616$, `Bon` at $0.0353$, and `Paris` at $0.0344$.

At $\tau=0.5$, the probability of `The` rises to $0.866$; at $\tau=2.0$, it falls to $0.026$. The manual eight-step loop, stopped by EOS, produces the same identifiers as `model.generate(do_sample=False)` and the text `The capital of France is Paris.`.

### C. Decoding strategies

For an open prompt about a robot discovering music, greedy, two temperature settings, top-k, and top-p produce five different continuations. For the capital-of-France prompt, every strategy answers `Paris` because the distribution is more concentrated. Two runs with `torch.manual_seed(42)` produce identical text.

### D. Format and history

Plain text without a chat template has 7 tokens and leads to immediate EOS and an empty response. The chat template creates 37 tokens including a system prompt and produces `The capital of France is Paris.`.

The history has 36 tokens after the first turn and 72 after the second. The model answers the name question only when the earlier message is included in the input.

### E. Cache and length

For 16 new tokens, runtime is 0.18 s with the cache and 0.44 s without it. For 128 tokens, the values are 1.29 s and 5.64 s. The advantage grows from about $2.5\times$ to $4.4\times$.

For prompts of 31 and 437 tokens followed by 32 generated tokens, runtime is 0.32 s and 0.47 s. Longer prefill raises the cost, but decode still dominates at this scale.

## Engineering implications

- Runtime grows with generated length because decode is sequential.
- The KV cache trades additional memory for lower runtime and grows linearly with length.
- GQA reduces memory through fewer K/V heads than Q heads.
- Prefill and decode have different cost profiles.
- A chat template is part of the model interface.
- Confusing `max_length` with `max_new_tokens` may truncate input or prevent generation.

## Key points

- The chain rule factors sequence probability into next-token distributions.
- Temperature changes distribution concentration and does not affect greedy decoding.
- Top-k retains a fixed number of tokens, while top-p retains a variable number.
- Tokens per word depend on the tokenizer and language.
- Prefill is parallel across positions, while decode is sequential.
- The KV cache stores K and V for the current run, not model knowledge.
- Chat templates and special tokens belong to the model contract.
- An instruct model differs through training, not architecture.
